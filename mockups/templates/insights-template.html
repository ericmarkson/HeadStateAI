Retrospective, descriptive, unscored — the scope decision-log.md D21 settled after narrowing D6. Every relationship on this screen describes what has already happened in the logged record; nothing predicts, scores, or reacts to today. Sleep/weather/medication-day co-occurrence is in scope now (needs nothing beyond build steps 1–4); anything keyed to a phase or prodrome label stays gated on step 5 and is called out below as deliberately absent, not missing.

Insights

Patterns already in your own log — sleep, weather, medication days — set beside the headache and migraine days you logged. Recomputed each time you open this page, over a fixed trailing window: 23 May–21 Aug 2026 (90 days).

What this page is — and isn’t
Is

A look back at your own record: how often a migraine day followed a night of short sleep, an overnight pressure drop, or a treated day, over the fixed window above.

Isn’t

A prediction, a risk score, or a “your migraine risk today” statement. Nothing on this screen is forward-looking, and nothing scores today or any day to come.

Correlation here means co-occurrence, never cause. “On days you did X, Y happened Z% of the time” is what this page shows — never “X causes Y” or “X is a trigger.”

HeadState waits for at least 8 qualifying days in the window before showing a percentage here, and this window doesn’t have that many yet. We deliberately don’t show the current count next to that threshold — a running tally against a target reads as a countdown to complete, which is exactly the “log a few more to see your pattern” nudge this screen is built not to be. The minimum itself is a product choice we made, not a clinical or statistical rule (the same kind of house decision as BACKFILL_WINDOW in CLAUDE.md). This card only ever reflects stress observations you already logged for their own reasons — it is not a prompt to log more.

◆ Deliberately absent: any correlation keyed to phase or prodrome labels — e.g. “symptom X preceded a migraine in Y% of cases.” That is D6's forward-looking, phase-dependent surface: narrowed by D21, not reopened. It stays out until step 5's retrospective phase annotation actually exists and has run for a while.

This page is exploratory, not validated. The largest trial of trigger-analytics layered on a headache diary (n=477, 12 weeks) found no significant benefit over a bare diary — self-monitoring itself appeared to drive the improvement in both arms. Insights is offered because some people find it useful to see their own record reflected back, not because doing so has been shown to change outcomes.
